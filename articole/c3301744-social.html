Paris police officers sentenced to prison over 2020 beating of Black man

Three French police officers were sentenced in Paris on 20 November 2026 for the violent assault of music producer Michel Zecler in November 2020. The Paris criminal court imposed five-year sentences with portions suspended, leaving between one and two years of imprisonment to be served. It also found that racism was an aggravating factor and permanently barred the three men from police service. The verdict closes an important stage of a case that became a national controversy over police violence and accountability.

The incident took place on 21 November 2020, during restrictions associated with the Covid-19 pandemic. Officers approached Zecler after observing him outside without a face covering, then followed him into his recording studio in Paris. Security-camera footage recorded a prolonged assault. The video was particularly important because the initial police account portrayed Zecler as the aggressor. Investigators subsequently examined the recordings, and the accusations originally directed against the producer were abandoned.

According to the account of the proceedings published by Le Monde, the footage showed repeated punches, baton strikes and kicks over several minutes. Zecler suffered injuries that resulted in 45 days of temporary incapacity for work. Police reinforcements later arrived at the studio, and the intervention affected other people present there. The trial therefore examined not only the conduct of the three defendants but also the wider response and the accuracy of official reporting.

The court found the defendants guilty of offences including intentional violence and falsification of an official document. An AFP report published after the verdict identified the sentences as five years, with three years suspended for Aurélien Lapersonne; five years, with three and a half suspended for Philippe Tondeux; and five years, with four suspended for Pierre Plantier. These distinctions matter: the headline figure of five years is not the same as the amount of immediate custodial time. The court also made different arrangements for enforcement of the custodial portions.

The finding on racist motivation was a central question during the hearings. Zecler consistently maintained that racial insults accompanied the assault; the defendants denied making them. Earlier investigating judges had not retained that aggravating circumstance, but the trial court ultimately accepted it, explaining its assessment in light of testimony and the credibility of the competing accounts. Reporting the finding as the court's conclusion is different from claiming that every disputed detail was independently established by journalists.

Prosecutors had sought longer immediate prison terms than those ultimately imposed. The judges considered the defendants' acknowledgements of wrongdoing and other personal circumstances when determining punishment, according to AFP. The officers apologised during the proceedings, while disputing aspects of the accusations. Their apologies did not erase the court's findings concerning the violence or the false report.

Beyond the individual convictions, the case illustrates why independent evidence and scrutiny of official records matter. Video evidence allowed investigators and judges to compare the written account with what actually happened inside the studio. It also raises continuing questions about supervision, the treatment of people subjected to police checks and the mechanisms available to challenge an inaccurate report. These are broader questions prompted by the judgment, not additional findings against other officers or the police institution as a whole.

The ruling was reported on 20 November 2026. Any appeal or subsequent change to the sentences would require separate confirmation; the information here describes the verdict as reported that evening.
