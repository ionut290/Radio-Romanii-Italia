Russian strike kills two, wounds 60, in Zelenskyy’s hometown

A missile attack on Kryvyi Rih in central Ukraine killed two people and injured 60, according to Ukrainian local and regional officials cited by the Associated Press and Al Jazeera on Saturday, November 21. The attack took place on Friday night in the hometown of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. The reported fatalities were a seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old woman. Six children were among the wounded. These are figures attributed to the authorities, not independently established casualty totals, and they could be revised as medical assessments continue.

Oleksandr Vilkul, the head of the city's administration, reported extensive damage to residential and civilian buildings. Approximately 25 apartment buildings, schools, a hospital, businesses, vehicles and an industrial facility were affected, according to the accounts published by the two news organisations. Four fires were reported. The destruction matters beyond the immediate death toll: damage to homes and public facilities can displace families and complicate access to treatment, schooling and basic services. The available reporting does not establish how many people have been displaced or the full cost of reconstruction.

Vilkul also alleged that additional missiles struck after emergency crews arrived at the scene. Al Jazeera reported that footage released by Ukrainian emergency services appeared to show further strikes during rescue operations. Such reports raise serious concerns for the safety of firefighters, medical teams and utility workers. However, the exact sequence, intended targets and circumstances of the additional impacts require careful independent investigation; the allegation alone does not establish intent to target rescuers.

The strike occurred during a wider period of intensified military and diplomatic tension. The Associated Press reported other attacks affecting civilians in the Kharkiv region and Kyiv, while Russian regional authorities separately reported injuries from Ukrainian drone strikes in border areas. These accounts concern different incidents and should not be combined into the Kryvyi Rih casualty figures. The broader war has continued despite diplomatic contacts involving Washington, Moscow and Kyiv.

A further point of concern for European security emerged after missiles and a suspected drone reportedly fell in Poland. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk described the timing as a possible provocation amid European military exercises. This is the Polish government's assessment, not a completed public determination of the objects' origins or intentions. NATO officials condemned the reported airspace violation. The events illustrate how military activity around Ukraine can generate risks and political pressure beyond its borders.

For the residents of Kryvyi Rih, the immediate priorities are rescue, medical care, securing damaged structures and establishing what happened. The political context does not diminish the need to distinguish confirmed damage from contested claims. Nor should the ongoing negotiations be presented as proof that a ceasefire is imminent. At the time of these reports, no verified agreement ending the fighting had been announced.

This RDI review draws on Al Jazeera's November 21 report, which credits AFP and AP, and the Associated Press account carried by ITV News. The principal casualty and damage figures originate from Ukrainian officials quoted in that coverage. Independent forensic findings and any subsequent changes to the casualty figures were not available in the material reviewed.
